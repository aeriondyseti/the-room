# Review — 6.9 Bounded shutdown (TD-0023) (v0.5.1)

- **Problem:**
  - Service shutdown awaited in-flight monitor and account observations (probes, bounded git sampling, Codex daemon read), so `room shutdown` could take seconds.
  - `server.test` "shutdown cleans ownership" (a 3s window) failed roughly every other full run under load.
- **Change:**
  - **Bounded stop:** `ParticipantMonitor.stop()` and `AccountMonitor.stop()` wait for an in-flight pass only within `monitorStopGraceMs` (default 500, `ROOM_MONITOR_STOP_GRACE_MS`).
  - **Abandoned passes:** past the grace, the pass is marked abandoned and writes, publishes and persists nothing.
  - **Passes that finish in time:** a pass that finishes within the grace still saves (observer cursors survive a restart; the first attempt broke this, and the existing integration test caught it).
  - **Concurrent stops:** `RoomService.close()` stops the observers concurrently.
  - **Prompt exit:** the detached service exits once fully closed (`startServer({ onClosed })`), so abandoned work can't keep the process and its handles alive. It exits 1 with a message when lock cleanup failed.
- **Lean review (ADR-0007 shape: 2 reviewers + 1 batched verifier, 3 agents):** complete: true, 1 actionable (reported twice), fixed:
  - `ROOM_MONITOR_STOP_GRACE_MS` was never honoured (the monitors fell back to the frozen default). It's now threaded from `loadConfig()` through `startServer` into both monitors, with a config test.
- **Tech-debt findings:**
  - The detached service exited 0 even when lock cleanup failed. Fixed: `onClosed` receives the error, and the process exits 1.
  - `ActivityService.stop()` is still awaited without a bound; it does local transcript reads, outside TD-0023's scope → TD-0026.
- **Tests:**
  - a hung probe or provider → `stop()` returns within the grace, and nothing is saved or published afterwards (monitor and accounts);
  - the config override.

  Full suite: 301/301 in each of 2 consecutive runs after the fixes. Before them, 4 consecutive runs were green (300/300), where the flake used to fail about every other run.
